Clinical data

Electronic health record extracts in FHIR, OMOP CDM and C-CDA, other clinical tables such as registries and patient-reported outcomes, consent tables, and clinical documents.

Clinical data standards

FHIR, OMOP CDM and C-CDA files are delivered together as a collection: a FHIR export or set of Bundles, an OMOP CDM instance, or a set of C-CDA documents. The collection declares the standard and its version, such as FHIR R4 with US Core 6.1, so the files do not repeat it. OMOP tables are the exception: each names its CDM version, since the table names depend on it.

FHIR

FHIR files have three forms. A Bulk Data export is NDJSON with one resource type per file, a FhirNdjsonFile. A Bundle, such as one patient's record from Synthea, is a FhirBundleFile; it lists its subjects, since a Bundle usually holds one person. A single resource that is not a Bundle is a FhirResourceFile. The resource type must exist in the FHIR version of the collection.

Device data already in FHIR form, such as Observations from a wearable, stays a FHIR file. Its collection declares the HL7 Personal Health Device IG alongside FHIR.

OMOP CDM

Each OMOP table uses an OmopTableFile with cdmTable and omopCdmVersion. The table must exist in that version. The CDM already defines OMOP foreign keys.

C-CDA

Each C-CDA document is a CcdaDocumentFile listing its subject. The document header carries its template IDs, and the document set gives the C-CDA release.

Other clinical tables

Registry, patient-reported outcome, claims and mortality tables that are not in a clinical standard are TabularFiles. The file's name and path identify the table. A data dictionary describing its columns is a DocumentationFile.

Consent

A ConsentFile records permitted data uses for each listed subject. The platform applies them to access decisions rather than processing them as clinical data.

Clinical documents

Notes, reports and scanned documents are ClinicalDocumentFiles. They are unstructured, so they are de-identified with a text scan, or OCR for scans, before use. Each lists its subject. Their containsFreeText flag is assumed true unless set otherwise. A scanned report can carry a processing hint for the OCR step; hints are informational only.

Sensitivity

Clinical classes, including FHIR, are not automatically classified as identifying. Their files may still contain identifying information and require de-identification checks.

  • Clinical data usually contains dates more precise than a year and may contain postal codes. Both are accepted at deposit and masked at release. Declare them with containsDates and containsZipCodes, or set them to false when the data has none.
  • Free text, in notes or in free-text fields of a structured table, needs a de-identification scan. Declare it with containsFreeText on the files that have it.
  • Direct identifiers such as names and medical record numbers must be removed before upload.
